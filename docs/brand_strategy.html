Keeper: Brand Strategy & Design Philosophy

Version: 1.0 Core Archetype: The Silent Guardian Tagline: "Keeper remembers, so you can sleep."


1. The Origin Story (The "Why")

The Problem: The Burden of Memory

Parenting is often sold as a series of joyous moments, but the reality for a new parent is a crushing weight of data.

  • "When did he eat?"
  • "Which breast?"
  • "Is he warm enough?"
  • "Did I give the medicine at 2:00 or 2:30?"

This is the Mental Load. It is invisible, it is exhausting, and it destroys sleep. When a parent is trying to remember data, they cannot be present with their child. They become data entry clerks in their own homes.

The Solution: "Keeper"

We chose the name Keeper because it implies two things:

  • Protection: "I am my brother's keeper." It implies safety, watching over, and guarding.
  • Memory: "The Keeper of the Record." It holds the history so you don't have to.

Our Mission: To liberate parents from the anxiety of remembering, allowing them to return to the joy of caring. We do not "track" babies; we "keep" the family's peace of mind.


2. Human-Centric Design Pillars

Our design must forgive the user for being tired. We assume the user is operating at 40% cognitive capacity.

Pillar 1: The "3:00 AM" Test

  • Context: The user is likely holding a screaming baby in one hand, in a pitch-black room, with one eye open.
  • Rule: If it requires two hands, it is broken. If it blinds the user with white light, it is aggressive.
  • Execution: Dark Mode is not a "setting"; it is the default soul of the app. Buttons are massive. Text is high-contrast.

Pillar 2: Competence over Cuteness

  • Context: Parents are inundated with "cutesy" apps (cartoons, storks, pastel pinks). This feels infantizing.
  • Rule: We treat parents like the CEOs of their home.
  • Execution: The interface looks like a professional tool (like Linear or Notion), but softened. It inspires confidence. It says, "I have this handled," not "Look how cute the baby is."

Pillar 3: Pessimistic Safety, Optimistic Support

  • Context: Medical anxiety is high.
  • Rule: When it comes to medicine, we are rigid (Red cards, warnings). When it comes to routine (sleep/food), we are fluid.
  • Execution: The AI is warm but precise. It never guesses on health data.


3. Visual Identity: "Nocturnal Softness"

We are designing for the night. The aesthetic is deep, cool, and bioluminescent.

A. The Logo: The Speaking Shield

  • Concept: A fusion of protection (Shield) and communication (Speech Bubble).
  • Style: Minimalist Line Art. Think "Swiss Design" meets "Apple Health."
  • Stroke: Constant width, rounded caps. Elegant, not cartoonish.
  • Meaning: "We protect you by listening to you."

B. Color Palette

We avoid "Pure Black" (#000000) because it creates "smearing" on OLED screens and high contrast strain. We use Deep Slates.

  • Background (The Void): Midnight Slate (#0F172A)
    • Usage: The main canvas. Deep, restful, infinite.
  • Primary Brand (The Trust): Moonlight Lavender (#818CF8)
    • Usage: The "Mic" button, active states, the "AI" voice. It glows against the dark background.
  • Secondary (The Text): Starlight Cream (#F1F5F9)
    • Usage: Primary text. Softened white to prevent eye burn.
  • Alert (The Guardian): Sunset Coral (#FB7185)
    • Usage: Medical warnings. It is urgent but warm, unlike "Traffic Light Red" which feels judgmental.

C. Typography

  • Font Family: Inter or Geist Sans.
  • Characteristics: Tall x-height, highly legible at small sizes, neutral character.
  • Sizing: We scale everything up by 10%. 16px is the new 14px.


4. Tone of Voice (The AI Persona)

The AI is Keeper. It is not a "Robot" (beep boop), and it is not a "Chatty Friend" (too much noise).

  • The Persona: An experienced Night Nurse.
    • Qualities: Calm, Brief, Alert, Warm.
  • Do's:
    • "Logged. 4oz at 2:00 PM." (Brief confirmation)
    • "That dose looks high. Please check." (Direct safety)
    • "You've been up for 3 hours. Try to rest." (Gentle nudges)
  • Don'ts:
    • "Hey mama! You're doing great! Here is the log!" (Too much energy)
    • "Data saved successfully." (Too robotic)


5. UI Heuristics (The "Thumb Zone")

  • Bottom-Up Navigation: All interactive elements (Input, Mic, Confirm) must live in the bottom 30% of the screen. The top 70% is for reading, not reaching.
  • The "Fat Finger" Standard: Minimum touch target size is 48x48pt. No tiny "x" buttons to close modals. Swipe-to-dismiss is preferred.
  • Haptic Reassurance: Every "Save" action must have a subtle haptic "thud." The parent needs physical confirmation that the memory is safe, so they can let go of it mentally.
